# Meta Campaign — /book National Booking Page

**Destination:** https://www.freshforlesscarpetcleaning.co.uk/book
**Built:** 21 August 2026 · **Pixel:** installed, now fires `Schedule` (confirmed) and `Lead` (provisional)

---

## Read this before you spend anything

**Your coverage is Wirral only.** Confirmed bookings are possible in CH41–CH49 and
CH60–CH63. Everywhere else the page takes a *provisional request* and promises
confirmation within 24 hours — which you can only honour if you're ready to ring
round and find a cleaner that day.

That single fact decides the campaign. Run this national and most of the budget
buys requests you can't fill, you break a 24-hour promise at scale, and Meta
learns to find you more of the same. **Campaign 1 is Wirral. Campaign 2 is a
small, deliberate national test you turn off if you can't service it.**

**Your other offer pages contradict this one.** `/4for99`, `/3for79` and `/3for49`
still advertise prices the booking engine won't honour. Don't run them alongside
this — a customer who sees both loses trust in the price.

needs to learn.

**Ad set A — Broad** (no detailed targeting)
**Ad set B — Homeowners** (Behaviours → Homeowners) — only if A stabilises first

### Retargeting ad set — £5/day
Custom audiences: `/book` visitors (30 days) who did **not** fire `Schedule`.
That's your highest-intent pocket — they priced up their house and hesitated.

## Campaign 2 — National test (optional, £10/day, kill it fast)

Same creative, `Lead` as the conversion event, location UK-wide excluding your
Wirral radius so the two don't compete. Judge it on one question only: **can you
actually fill the requests it generates?** If you're not converting them into
confirmed jobs within 24 hours, switch it off — it's costing you reputation,
not just money.

## Creative

Static images will carry this — the offer is the hook, not the production.

1. **Price card.** Plain background, huge "£99", subtext "3 rooms cleaned · price fixed online". Your logo bottom right. This will almost certainly win.
2. **Before/after split.** A genuinely grubby carpet beside the clean one. Shoot it on a real job — stock images read as fake and kill trust for local services.
3. **Phone screenshot.** The `/book` page mid-quote showing the price updating. Sells the mechanism, not the clean.
4. **Video, 15s.** Postcode typed → items ticked → price appears → slot chosen. Silent with captions; most people watch muted.

Run 1, 2 and 3 in every ad set. Add 4 once you have a winner to beat.

## Budget and what to expect

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Start | £20/day Wirral + £5/day retargeting |
| Expected CPM | £6–12 (UK local, small radius) |
| Expected CPC | £0.60–1.50 |
| Landing → booking | 4–10% on a page this direct |
| Expected cost per booking | £12–30 |
| Average job value | ~£99–150 |

At 17.5% you keep about £17–26 a job, so **paid acquisition does not pay for
itself on commission alone.** That's the uncomfortable arithmetic and it's worth
sitting with: it only works if you take the work yourself in Wirral, or if
repeat business follows. Track it honestly before you scale.

## Before you switch it on

1. Confirm the pixel fires — book a test job and check Events Manager for `Schedule`.
2. Pause the conflicting `/3for79`, `/3for49` and `/4for99` ads.
3. Have someone ready to respond to provisional requests within 24 hours, or don't run Campaign 2.
4. **Cookie consent.** The pixel fires before anyone agrees to anything. That's the one part of this with real regulatory exposure.

## Reading it after a week

Ignore CPM and CTR. The only numbers that matter are **cost per `Schedule`** and
whether those bookings actually got completed. A cheap booking that cancels is
worse than no booking — it costs you a slot and a cleaner's goodwill.

Give it 7 days and at least 50 link clicks per ad set before judging anything.
Turning ads off on day two is the most common way to waste a Meta budget.
